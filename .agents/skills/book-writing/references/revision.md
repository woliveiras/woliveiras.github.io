# Revise a technical manuscript

## Start with the reader's path

Read the promise, the description of the audience, and the table of contents. Open the first and last passages of each chapter and find the passages that support the stated dependencies. Trace where each skill is introduced, practiced, and revisited. Compare what the opening offers with what the conclusion demonstrates. Try entering through the table of contents, a term, or a task. Check whether readers can tell if they are ready to apply what they find.

Prioritize issues by their effect on the reader.

1. **Book structure.** Find the first use of each essential idea. Check that readers encounter its definition and prerequisites before they need it. Move or split material when the order obstructs learning. Keep independent reading paths when they give readers enough to begin.
2. **Chapter.** Compare the opening, development, and close. Mark where the chapter promises an ability, teaches it, shows evidence, and gives readers a chance to practice. Bring the opening into line with what the chapter delivers or complete the promised work. Check that the transition to the next chapter identifies a real need.
3. **Passage.** Identify gaps between the claim, example, and interpretation. Reorder passages if readers need a condition before acting. When a series of disconnected sentences makes the reasoning hard to follow, group them around the decision and spell out their relationships. Check for consistency in how the author addresses readers, uses terms and units, and expresses certainty. Preserve the features of the author's voice that help readers.
4. **Reference use and consistency.** Try finding a definition without its exact wording. Follow a cross-reference from the point where readers make a decision and confirm that the destination contains what it promises. Check the headings, tables, diagrams, captions, listings, glossaries, appendices, exercise answers, and indexes the manuscript uses. When editing a recurring example, track its state, values, and names through the chapters that use it again.

## Make edits that can be verified

For an editorial report, locate each finding by chapter and passage. Describe the effect on the reader, the proposed edit, and what should be true after it is made. Group issues that share a cause. When delivering revised prose, preserve the technical meaning, the useful qualities of the author's voice, and the established conventions. Identify assumptions or data that need the author's confirmation.

After moving or renaming a section, reread the preceding close and the following opening. Follow affected cross-references and check that each destination answers the question it promises to address. When revising a demonstration, compare the explanation with the values, states, and conditions shown in outputs, tables, or figures.

**Original example.** In the ceramics shipment check, the second paragraph asks readers to confirm the link between the record and photograph before explaining where the number comes from. Add a one-sentence reminder there and point to the section that teaches readers how to retrieve the record. The edit is complete when someone arriving from the table of contents can find the record from the photograph and knows where to review the full procedure.

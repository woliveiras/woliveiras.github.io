# Write technical books

Start with what readers will be able to explain, do, or decide by the end of the book. Relate that outcome to what they already know and when they will use the book. Let the promise guide what to teach, in what order, and at what depth.

Use the requested language or the language of the manuscript. Work from the brief, the technical material, and any editorial decisions already made. Distinguish established facts and decisions from editorial assumptions. When context is missing, proceed with assumptions you can state and revise. Identify the specific gap when it prevents a reliable decision.

## Choose the workflow for the deliverable

- To plan the book, define the reader promise, the chapter progression, and the ways readers can look things up. Deliver an annotated table of contents that shows what each chapter teaches and where readers will use it. [Planning](references/planning.md)
- To draft a chapter, locate it in the book's progression, establish what readers already know, and develop the promised skill or understanding. Deliver the requested prose at the agreed level of polish. [Drafting](references/drafting.md)
- To revise a manuscript, test chapter dependencies and overall structure first. Then examine how chapters teach, how the voice holds together, and how readers find material again. Apply the requested edits or report findings tied to specific passages. [Revision](references/revision.md)

For tasks that combine workflows, consult the relevant guides as needed. For decisions about glossaries, appendices, indexes, and cross-references, see the section on reference use in the planning guide.

When preparing a work plan for the author, tie each recommendation to a passage or decision, explain its intended effect on the reader, and say how to verify the result.

Keep terminology, the state of recurring examples, and cross-references aligned across passages. When a definition, step, or result changes, check where it is used again. Preserve the source material's technical claims and flag specific factual uncertainties for verification.
